I took a computer class as an undergraduate, but I didn't use programming in research until my master's degree. There I assembled ~100 kb chloroplast genomes from Illumina short reads, which meant learning Linux, Bash and scripting along the way. I enjoyed it so much that I chose bioinformatics for my PhD.

When I started my PhD at the University of Florida, my advisor handed me [*Sams Teach Yourself Perl in 21 Days*](https://books.google.com/books?vid=ISBN9780672320354). After working through it, I jumped into real projects, and I have believed ever since that learning by doing is the best way into bioinformatics and genomics. [BioPerl](https://bioperl.org/) had many useful examples for processing and analyzing data. The field moves fast, and searching for answers online was my best teacher along the way.

I picked up more Python and R as my PhD progressed. The choice of language often depends on the lab; my postdoc lab worked mostly in R. Today, [Biopython](https://biopython.org/) and R's [Bioconductor](https://www.bioconductor.org/) both offer packages for large-scale biological data analysis. For machine learning, Python has [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/), and *[An Introduction to Statistical Learning](https://www.statlearning.com/)* is an excellent book with labs in both R and Python.

Here are a few more resources I found useful.

## Bioinformatics in general

- [*Bioinformatics Data Skills*](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/bioinformatics-data-skills/9781449367480/) by Vince Buffalo

## R

- [Posit (RStudio) cheat sheets](https://posit.co/resources/cheatsheets/)
- [*R for Data Science*](https://r4ds.hadley.nz/) by Hadley Wickham and colleagues

## Python

- [*Python Data Science Handbook*](https://jakevdp.github.io/PythonDataScienceHandbook/) by Jake VanderPlas

## Community

- [Capital of Statistics (统计之都)](https://cosx.org/), a Chinese-language statistics and data science community
