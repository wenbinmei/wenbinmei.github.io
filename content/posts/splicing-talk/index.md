The [Chinese Genomics Online Meet-up](http://chinesegenomics.weebly.com/) is a self-organized group of young Chinese researchers, mainly PhD students, postdocs and assistant professors, who meet online every two weeks to discuss plant genomics, evolution, statistics, bioinformatics, genetics and more.

I gave a research talk there, *The Cause and Consequence of Alternative Splicing in Maize and Across Species*, covering the first two chapters of my PhD dissertation.

The first part of the talk presents a genome-wide analysis of alternative splicing in maize, published as [A Comprehensive Analysis of Alternative Splicing in Paleopolyploid Maize](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2017.00694) in *Frontiers in Plant Science* (2017).

The second part asks which alternative splicing events are evolutionarily conserved, and therefore likely under functional constraint, across monocots, focusing on five grass species: maize, sorghum, rice, foxtail millet and *Brachypodium*. Many alternative splicing variants are probably neutral; alongside classic mutant studies, our approach offers a way to identify potentially functional splicing events. This work was published as [Evolutionarily Conserved Alternative Splicing Across Monocots](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.117.300189) in *Genetics* (2017), where it was the October 2017 cover article.

Both papers are listed as outputs of NSF Plant Genome Research Program award [#1547787](https://www.nsf.gov/awardsearch/show-award/?AWD_ID=1547787), for which this dissertation work provided the preliminary data.
